Executive summary

Audit overview
Public agencies and institutions of higher education in Washington are permitted
to use credit cards to improve efficiency, flexibility and convenience when buying
goods and services. However, state policies established by the Department of
Enterprise Services (DES) limit what can be purchased with credit cards.
In fiscal year 2012, the six four-year colleges and universities in Washington used
credit cards to buy more than $164 million in goods and services.
We performed an audit to determine if the three universities that made more than
95 percent ($157 million) of all purchases, had effective internal controls in place
over the use of credit cards.
The three universities we audited were:
   • The University of Washington (UW)
   • Washington State University (WSU)
   • Western Washington University (WWU)
We reviewed $7,915,552 in purchases – five percent of all the purchases the three
universities made with their cards in fiscal year 2012.

In most cases, the purchases we audited were made for allowable purposes and the

do not allow, such as alcohol and gifts. The cost of the unallowable purchases

While performing our audit to determine if credit cards were
only used to make allowable purchases, other issues came to our
attention.
We found:
     • The three universities used credit cards to make purchases that were not
       supported by adequate documentation. For example, in fiscal year 2012 the
       universities paid more than $4.5 million for food and beverages. For the
       purchases we tested where food and beverages were attributed to business
       meetings, supporting documents did not adequately describe why the food
       and beverages were integral to the meetings.
     • Instances when non-discretionary funds1 were used to pay for purchases
       that were not allowable according to universities policies. For example,
       the UW used credit cards to purchase more than $63,000 with its
       non-discretionary funds for decorative and gift items and to pay for the
       cost of meals that exceeded the state-allowed per diem. University policy
       stipulates these expenses must be paid for with discretionary funds. We
       also found instances when university policies were lacking, unclear and/or
       contradictory.

Recommendations
We recommend the universities use credit cards only to make purchases that are
allowable according to state policies.
We make other recommendations regarding how each of the universities can
improve compliance with other state and university policies, improve internal
controls and promote accountability over the use of public resources.

Introduction

Audit authority
We performed this audit under the authority of state law (RCW 43.09.310), which
requires the State Auditor to perform post-audits of state agencies. These audits are
designed to assess whether agencies have systems in place to ensure accountability
over public funds and comply with state laws and policies. Educational institutions
are included in the definition of a state agency (RCW 43.09.290).
About the state credit card program
Public agencies and institutions of higher education in Washington are permitted
to use credit cards to improve their efficiency, flexibility and convenience when
buying goods and services. However, there are limits on what they can buy with
these cards.
The Department of Enterprise Services (DES), has established a credit card
program with policies and standards2 public agencies and institutions of higher
education must follow when using them. State law3 authorizes DES to establish
these policies and standards.
While state law4 grants colleges and universities some flexibility to independently
exercise certain powers otherwise granted to the director of DES, it does not
exempt them from policies and standards of the state credit card program.
These state policies and standards describe both acceptable and unacceptable uses
of credit cards. Examples of purchases that are unacceptable include:
   • Alcoholic beverages and liquor licenses
   • Gifts and donations
   • Inter-department purchases
   • Personal purchases
A complete list of what state credit cards may and may not be used to purchase is
presented in Appendix A of the report.
Public agencies and institutions of higher education may devise more restrictive,
but not less restrictive, policies regarding what may be purchased with their state
credit cards. In planning our audit, we considered any additional restrictions put
in place by the universities.

Universities and colleges pay their credit card bills using discretionary
and non-discretionary funds
Colleges and universities in Washington receive a small percentage of their annual
operating budgets directly from the state general fund. The majority of their
operating revenues come from enterprise functions, such as UW Medicine and
athletic programs. Other sources of operating revenue include: student tuition and
fees and federal and state grants. A university’s use of its public, non-discretionary
funds is limited to the authority established in law, and by granting agencies or
specific directives of donors.
Unlike most public agencies, universities and colleges also receive funds from
private donations. At times, donors give money with specific purposes in mind,
restricting the use of their gifts. Donors also give funds without placing any
limitations on how the money should be spent. This type of gift money is classified
by the universities as “discretionary” and is used for such purposes as the Board of
Regents (or President, Dean, etc.) deems to be in the best interest of the university
or college.
Institutions of higher education use their credit cards to buy goods and services
paid for by both non-discretionary funds and discretionary funds. Although the
institutions use discretionary funds to pay for things non-discretionary funds
cannot, state policies5 limit what credit cards can be used to buy.

Audit scope and objective
We limited the audit scope to the following three universities that spent the most
with credit cards in state fiscal year 2012:
   • University of Washington ($119.4 million)
   • Washington State University ($32.8 million)
   • Western Washington University ($5.7 million)
We designed the audit to determine whether these universities had effective
internal controls in place to ensure credit cards are only used for allowable
purposes. Each university’s results are presented in separate sections because they
operate independently of one another.
Methodology
We selected which cardholders and purchases to review using a risk-based approach.
The following are examples of the criteria we used to make our selections:
  • Cardholders who spent the most
  • Uncommon merchant names
  • Round amounts (i.e. $25, $50, $100)
  • Transactions with questionable, vague or blank purchase descriptions
We visited each university, conducted interviews with staff and reviewed
supporting documentation for credit card purchases.
To determine what state laws and policies pertain to credit card use by institutions
of higher education, we consulted with staff from OFM and our legal advisors.

Audit results

University of Washington
Summary of audit results
In most cases, the credit card purchases we audited were made for allowable
purposes and the UW had effective internal controls in place. However, we found
some UW credit cards were used to make $197,265 in purchases that were not
allowable according to state policies. We also found areas where the university can
improve its internal controls, compliance with other state and internal policies
and accountability in the use of public resources.
Audit overview
The University of Washington has established its credit card program for efficiency
purposes, to reduce its reliance on petty cash funds and to eliminate the use of
personal funds.
In state fiscal year 2012, UW employees used credit cards to buy more than $119
million in goods and services – roughly 72 percent of all card purchases by the
four-year institutions of higher education. As of June 30, 2012, UW had 3,154
active cards.
Exhibit 3 shows the scope of our audit: We examined transactions made by
87 cardholders and reviewed supporting documentation for 3.5 percent of all
purchases made by the university.

Internal controls
The UW credit card program is administered by the Procurement Services
Department. It offers mandatory training for new credit card users and ongoing
training for existing users. The Department has also established internal policies
and procedures that describe the roles and responsibilities of card holders and
reviewers. The responsibility for the use of credit cards rests with the cardholders,
reconcilers and administrators within departments across the university.
The Procurement Card Services team conducts monthly reviews of high-risk
purchases and contacts the cardholder and/or department administrator for
additional information. When appropriate, the team advises departments on what
corrective measures should be taken and educate the user about the applicable
policies and procedures. Purchases that are considered high risk are food, fuel for
vehicles, travel and those made through PayPal. Additionally, UW has maintained
a maximum purchase limit of $3,500, which is significantly below the direct buy
limit established by state policies. Any purchases over this limit must receive prior
authorization from the Procurement Services Department.

The Procurement Services Department creates reports for the high-risk purchases
it identifies, which are available to all managers across the university. Each
month the Department identifies between 150 and 200 purchases and reports this

UW uses discretionary funds to pay for some credit card charges
The UW uses credit cards to buy goods and services paid for with both
non-discretionary and discretionary funds. The University has not implemented
institution-wide policies that describe what card holders can purchase with
discretionary funds. Reliance is placed on individual departments across the
University to manage how their discretionary funds are spent.
In fiscal year 2012, approximately $5.3 million (4.4 percent) of all UW credit card
purchases were paid for with its discretionary funds.

Alcohol and liquor licenses
Credit cards may not be used to buy alcoholic beverages and liquor licenses. We

baskets and flower arrangements.
Inter-department purchases
Purchases between internal departments of an agency may not be made with
credit cards, unless it has already obtained written approval from the OFM. We
found UW spent $5,838 on inter-department purchases without approval.

Purchase of meals and light refreshments for meetings
State policies allow public agencies and institutions of higher education to provide
meals and light refreshments at meetings and training sessions, only when these
conditions are met:
   • The purpose of the meeting is to conduct state business or provide formal
      training that benefits the state.
   • The food is an integral part of the meeting.
   • A receipt for the actual costs of the food is obtained.
   • The meeting takes place away from the employee’s or official’s regular
      workplace.
   • The purchase is approved by an agency head, or designee, before the event.

In addition to this state policy, the University has implemented internal policies
that further restrict when employees may purchase food for meetings.
For the purchases we audited, documentation was not adequate for us to determine
whether serving food and light refreshments was integral to the meetings or
training sessions. In state fiscal year 2012, UW used its credit cards to buy more
than $3.3 million for meals with meetings or light refreshments paid for with
non-discretionary funds.
Hosting activities
UW has multiple policies that describe when departments can purchase food for
hosting, entertaining and social activities and what funds may be used to pay for
these expenses. Examples of these activities for which food was purchased were:
   • Employee birthdays
   • Retirement celebrations
   • Holiday parties (i.e. Christmas)
   • Department and donor receptions
Of the $4.1 million of UW credit card transactions we reviewed, $267,268 was
purchased with its non-discretionary funds to buy food that appeared to be served
during a hosting activity.
We found the university policies that describe the allowable uses of non-discretionary
funds for hosting activities were unclear and contradictory in some instances. This
condition could lead to uncertainty and inconsistencies by departments and staff
when trying to determine if an expense is allowable.
Inadequate supporting documentation for sponsorships
Some credit cards were used to sponsor external activities and events of charitable
and not-for-profit organizations. Organizations UW made payments to include: the
American Cancer Society, Group Health Foundation, Puget Sound Blood Center
and the Humane Society.
One UW department (UW Medicine) has a policy that describes the requirements
for its sponsorship arrangements. In part, the requirements stipulate:
   • UW must receive meaningful value for sponsorship payments.
   • The benefits received must be reasonably scaled to the size of the payment it
       makes.
   • The specific consideration received from the sponsorship must be fully
       documented in writing.
We examined invoices and other supporting documents for a selection of
organizations UW paid with credit cards and found:
   • Three instances when invoices from the organizations stated that the value
       of the goods and services it provided was less than the payment amount it
       received from UW. The difference between the value of the consideration
       UW received and the amount paid was $8,300. The organizations
       characterized these differences as tax deductible, charitable contributions.
   • Eleven other instances, totaling $64,750, when documentation was not
       adequate for us to conclude whether:
       • UW received meaningful value for payments it made to organizations; or
       • The benefit(s) UW received were reasonably scaled to the size of the
          payment.

Purchases not supported by receipts
Public agencies and institutions of higher education must maintain documentation
to support their purchases. In 2011, UW applied for and was granted a partial
waiver of this requirement by OFM. The university is only required to retain
supporting documentation for purchases costing $75 or more.
We found 88 credit card purchases that exceeded $75 and were not supported by
original or itemized receipts. These purchases cost $133,553.
Inaccurate coding for purchases
We noted 668 purchases totaling $483,166 that had inaccurate account coding.
State policies7 require public agencies and institutions of higher education to
properly classify transactions. To meet this requirement, account codes are
established to ensure management has an accurate record of purchases by class.
This information may be used for budget and planning purposes.
Accurate coding is also critical for items such as laptops, which may not meet
capitalization thresholds. These types of assets are considered attractive items
for which inventory and tracking is required. We did not test the University’s
procedures over property management as part of this audit.
Other credit card uses
We identified credit card purchases totaling $15,683 when supporting
documentation did not clearly show how the educational, research or service
mission of the University was met. UW paid for most of these purchases with its
discretionary funds.
Regular espresso expenses for graduate students
Espresso coffee and a milk delivery service costing $6,652 in one year were
purchased with a credit card. These items were used to supply an espresso machine
used by UW graduate students.
Department holiday party
In December 2011, a credit card was used to pay $2,344 for the catering expenses
of a department holiday party.
Department Chair’s birthday celebration
In June 2012 a credit card was used to pay $2,258 for food, decorations and rentals
for a Department Chair’s birthday celebration.
Columbia Tower Club membership
Monthly membership dues totaling $2,031 were paid for an employee to join the
Columbia Tower Club, which is a premier private business and social club located
in downtown Seattle.
Seattle Storm season tickets
A UW athletic department credit card was used to pay for season tickets to the
Seattle Storm, a professional women’s basketball team. The tickets cost $1,296.

Other activities more social than business in nature
Some departments used credit cards to make $1,102 in purchases for activities that
were more social than business in nature. Examples include food and decorations
for:
   • Birthdays
   • Baby showers
   • Staff retirement and going away celebrations
The UW does not have a University-wide policy that defines what can be purchased
with its discretionary funds. We believe such a policy would provide clarity to
departments regarding what the Board of Regents and University leadership
consider an acceptable use of these funds.

Recommendations
We recommend the University of Washington:
  • Use credit cards only to buy goods and services that are allowable
     according to state policies.
  • Use non-discretionary funds to pay only for goods and services that are
     allowable according to state and university policies.
  • Improve its documentation to support why serving food and light
     refreshments is integral to meetings.
  • Use high-risk purchase reports to monitor credit card purchases.
  • Review transactions more thoroughly to ensure they are properly coded.
  • Clarify its policies that describe when hosting activities are allowable
     university expenses.
  • Sponsor activities of other entities only where the University can document
     value to the university reasonably scaled to the amount of the University’s
     expenditure.
  • Implement a University-wide policy that establishes rules or parameters
     in how departments can spend discretionary funds. These policies should
     provide clarity and more consistency in how cardholders and departments
     use these funds.

Internal controls
The WSU credit card program is administered and maintained by the following
Departments:

We noted 193 purchases totaling $23,431 that had inaccurate account coding. State

We found five purchases for gift cards totaling $331 without supporting
documentation to show who received them.

We recommend Western Washington University:

  • More thoroughly review transactions to ensure they are properly coded.
  • Implement a University-wide policy that establishes rules or parameters in

Procurement Method
