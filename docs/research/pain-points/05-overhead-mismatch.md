# 05. A foundation dollar isn't worth the same as a federal dollar

**Status:** core
**One-liner:** Federal grants pay universities 30–60% extra for overhead;
foundations often cap it at 10–15%, so "the same amount" from a foundation
can leave the university unable to afford hosting the work.

## What happens (plain language)
Grants pay for the research (direct costs) and the university's overhead
(buildings, power, safety, admin: indirect costs). Replacing an NIH grant
dollar-for-dollar with foundation money usually means far less overhead.

## Everyday analogy
Swapping a job that pays salary + benefits for one with the same salary and
no benefits.

## Evidence
- Negotiated university indirect rates typically 30–60% (another source:
  25–70%). [verified] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12152721/ · https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48540
- Gates caps indirects at 10% for universities (the notice also says 15% in
  one place, inconsistent); Rockefeller 15%. Per NIH's own notice, which used
  these to justify its cuts. [verified] https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-25-068.html
- NIH's attempted 15% cap (NOT-OD-25-068, Feb 2025) was permanently enjoined
  2025-04-04; current status not re-checked. [verified]
  https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/nih-issues-guidance-capping-grant-indirect-cost-rates
